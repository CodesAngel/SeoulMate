Actor pages: current and proposed

Today a cast name on a drama page runs a Discover search for that name. The search finds the right dramas, but it shows them as search results: best match first, a "Fresh mix" button, and nothing about the person. The proposal gives every actor, director and writer a page of their own with all their dramas, how many you've seen, where to start, and who they work with. All of it comes from catalog tables that already exist (people, drama_credits, dramas). Use the buttons above each mock to switch between phone and computer, states, and themes.

1. The person page like an IMDb or MyDramaList name page, without photos

  • Start here is the person's highest-rated lead role (Daily Dose of Sunshine, 8.8).
  • No "Seen" badges and no Not seen filter: there's no watch history to use.
  • Follow is shown but asks the visitor to sign in.
  • You've watched 3 of 9 counts dramas in your list marked Completed or Watching.
  • Start here becomes the highest-rated lead role you haven't seen (Our Unwritten Seoul, 8.7).
  • Cards you've seen get a green Seen badge; Not seen 6 filters to the rest. Try the chips.
  • Click Follow in the mock to see the next state.
  • The button turns into Following (click again to unfollow).
  • A note explains what following does: new dramas with this person show up in a Home row, "New from people you follow".

What changes

  1. A page per person at /people/park-bo-young (the name in kebab case; people.name is unique, so slugs can't clash yet). The browser title is "Park Bo Young dramas (9) | SeoulMate".
  2. Header: role, count and year range as an eyebrow; the name at the page-title size (--fs-page); "Known for" = the 3 dramas with the most watchers; three chips: average rating (8.3), lead roles (9 of 9) and the most frequent network (tvN, 4). A collage of their 3 best-known posters instead of a photo: the catalog has no photos, and scraping photos of real people raises rights questions.
  3. Progress card (signed in): seen count from your list, a bar, and Start here = the highest-rated lead role you haven't seen. Guests get the highest-rated lead role and a line saying what signing in adds.
  4. Dramas grid: the site's existing drama card, with the year added to the sub line ("2025 · 12 eps"), a Seen badge, newest first. Filters are genre chips built from this person's own dramas (only genres with 2 or more: Romance 6, Thriller 3), plus Not seen when signed in. Sort: Newest, Highest rated, Most watched.
  5. Often works with: people who share 2 or more dramas with this person, from cast (top 6 billed), directors and writers. For Park Bo Young: Lee Jung Eun (actor) and Yoo Je Won (director).
  6. Directors and writers: everyone else who made these dramas, one line each, 5 shown, then "Show all".
  7. Layout: on a computer, the grid (4 columns) on the left and a 330 px side column on the right. On a phone, one column: collage above the name, buttons full width, the filter chips scroll sideways, a 2-column grid, then the side cards.

2. The links that lead there

People pages are only useful if they can be reached. Two places link to them: every cast, director and writer tag on a drama page, and actor searches on Discover.

  • Cast, Director and Writer tags link to people pages instead of /discover?q=. Each tag shows how many dramas that person has in a small pill.
  • New Writer row: the data has screenwriters, but the page doesn't show them today.
  • On a computer, hovering a name for 300 ms shows a small card (two posters, count, how many you've seen, a link). The mock shows it open on Park Bo Young. Phones skip the card; a tap opens the page.
  • When the search is read as an actor search (it already is: "Understood as actor based"), a person bar appears above the results with a link to the page.
  • The results don't change. The bar is display only, so search accuracy and the evaluation numbers are unaffected.
  • The response needs one extra field with the matched person's name; the analyzer already finds it.

Details

  • Data: the page reads people, drama_credits and dramas from Supabase REST on the server, the same way lib/catalog-server.ts does, cached for a day. No backend change for the page itself.
  • Lead or supporting comes from billing_order: 1–2 lead, 3–6 supporting; 7 and later aren't shown as cards (cameos). All 9 of Park Bo Young's roles are billed 1–2, so a Lead/Supporting filter would be useless here; it appears only when both sides have dramas.
  • Following: a new table, person_follows (user_id, person_id, created_at), with RLS like watchlist_items; PUT/DELETE /me/follows/{person_id} and GET /me/follows on the /me router.
  • SEO: people with 3 or more dramas (about 1,400) go into sitemap.ts with Person JSON-LD.
  • Risk, duplicate spellings: Park Seo Joon and Park Seo Jun would get two pages (issue #4 item 2). Fix that first.
  • Risk, same name: two different people with one name are merged. "Lee Hyung Min, 13 dramas" may be more than one director. The proper fix is storing MyDramaList person IDs in the scraper.

Cost

  • A page is 3 small REST reads, cached for a day: the person, their credits with drama rows, and the shared credits for "Often works with" (at most a few hundred rows).
  • Nothing runs on the search server, and the search results don't change, so there's no evaluator re-run for the page. The Discover bar adds one field to the response.
  • The sitemap grows from 2,133 to about 3,500 URLs.
  • Size M: about 2–3 sessions, plus the spelling fix.
