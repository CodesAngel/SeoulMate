Airing now and calendar: current and proposed

Today the site doesn't know what's on air. My list has an episode counter for dramas you're watching, but it can't tell you a new episode came out. The proposal shows this week's episodes, highlights the ones on your list, and offers a private calendar link so the phone reminds you. Episode dates are estimated from each drama's start date and airing days, and always labelled "expected".

1. This week like a TV guide, only for your dramas

  • A banner on top when a drama on your list has an episode today, with Watched Ep 9 to move your progress on.
  • On a computer: a 7-day grid, today outlined. On a phone: a strip of days (dots mark days with episodes, pink for your list) and that day's episodes below. Tap the days in the mock.
  • The days come from the real catalog: Love in Disguise airs Mon/Tue (Ep 9–10 this week), 100 Days of Deception Sat/Sun (Ep 13–14), Training the Intern Sundays (Ep 8).
  • Coming soon is empty, honestly: no drama in the catalog starts in the next 30 days, because the catalog is refreshed by hand.
  • The same banner sits above the home hero, only when something on your list airs today.
  • On My list, airing dramas get a badge: Ep 9 out today (pink) when the expected count is ahead of your progress, or Airing · Ep 13 on Sat (grey) for planned ones.
  • Everything else on the board is unchanged.

What changes

  1. A new page, /airing: the "new tonight" banner, the week, Coming soon, and the calendar link.
  2. Home gets the banner above the hero on days something on your list airs.
  3. My list gets the two badges on airing dramas.
  4. Private calendar link (an .ics feed) for Google or Apple Calendar: expected episode days for your Watching and Plan to watch dramas. Reminders without email.

Details

  • What the data has: aired gives start and end dates, so "on air" = start ≤ today ≤ end.
  • What it lacks: airing weekdays. MyDramaList pages have "Aired On" (for example "Monday, Tuesday"); the scraper saves those pages, so it needs one new field, carried into a new column dramas.air_days. The mock guesses the days from the start date and episode count.
  • Gaps in the data today: many 2026 dramas have only a start date ("Oct 4, 2026 - ?") and no network (publisher is blank for them), so the page can't show channels yet.
  • Freshness: airing data goes stale within days. The refresh scrape needs a schedule (the nightly catalog job, still open), at least for airing and upcoming dramas.
  • Endpoints: GET /airing?week=, GET /airing/upcoming, and GET /me/calendar.ics?token= with a per-user secret token (new column profiles.calendar_token, resettable).
  • Badges need watchlist_items.episodes_watched, the same column as Wrapped.

Cost

  • Catalog reads only; a week's grid is a few rows.
  • The real cost is keeping data fresh: a scheduled scrape, a scraper change and date logic.
  • No search or ranking change.
  • Size L. It's only as good as the scrape schedule, which is why it's last.

To decide

  1. Build the nightly catalog refresh first (needed here anyway), then this page?
  2. Show channels and streaming services once publisher is filled in for new dramas?
  3. The calendar link: worth it at launch, or later?
